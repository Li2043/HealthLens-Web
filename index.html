Everyday wellbeing

Wellbeing Tips

Simple, non-medical ideas for self-reflection and everyday student wellbeing.

  • 01

    Track patterns, not perfection

    Notice what affects your mood or energy over time. Small patterns can help you plan rest, study, and social time more realistically.

  • 02

    Ask for help early

    You do not need to wait until things feel overwhelming. Student support services exist for everyday concerns as well as urgent situations.

  • 03

    Break tasks into smaller steps

    Large deadlines and life admin feel more manageable when you split them into clear, achievable actions with short breaks in between.

  • 04

    Use trusted resources for serious concerns

    For ongoing or serious wellbeing concerns, speak to your university support team or trusted NHS resources rather than relying on general online content.

Inclusive design

Accessibility Statement

This project aims to follow WCAG-informed practices so student support content is clear and usable for everyone.

  • Semantic HTML — landmarks such as header, nav, main, section, article, and footer structure the page logically.
  • Clear heading hierarchy — headings follow a meaningful order from h1 to h3.
  • Visible labels — every form field has an associated label; placeholders are supplementary only.
  • Keyboard-friendly interactions — navigation, filters, accordion, and forms work with keyboard alone.
  • Visible focus states — links, buttons, and form controls show a clear focus indicator.
  • Readable contrast — text and interactive elements use accessible colour contrast.
  • Responsive layout — content adapts for mobile, tablet, and desktop screen sizes.
  • Clear non-medical wording — content focuses on wellbeing support and signposting, not diagnosis or treatment.
