Everyday wellbeing

Wellbeing Tips

Simple, non-medical ideas for self-reflection and everyday student wellbeing.

  • 01

    Track patterns, not perfection

    Notice what affects your mood or energy over time. Small patterns can help you plan rest, study, and social time more realistically.

    Read NHS self-help guidance
  • 02

    Ask for help early

    You do not need to wait until things feel overwhelming. Student support services exist for everyday concerns as well as urgent situations.

    View University wellbeing services
  • 03

    Break tasks into smaller steps

    Large deadlines and life admin feel more manageable when you split them into clear, achievable actions with short breaks in between.

    Read NHS student stress tips
  • 04

    Use trusted resources for serious concerns

    For ongoing or serious wellbeing concerns, speak to your university support team or trusted NHS resources rather than relying on general online content.

    Browse NHS mental health resources

Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers about how to use HealthLens and what it can — and cannot — offer.

No. HealthLens is a student project designed to help you discover wellbeing and support resources. It does not diagnose conditions, predict health outcomes, or recommend treatments. Always seek professional support for medical or mental health concerns.

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 999. For urgent mental health support in the UK, you can contact NHS 111 or visit your nearest A&E. Your university may also offer an out-of-hours wellbeing helpline — check their official website for current contact details.

Start with the urgency level on each card. Low urgency items may suit self-guided resources or peer groups. Medium urgency may benefit from speaking to a tutor, adviser, or wellbeing team. High urgency cards point toward immediate or specialist support. When unsure, contact your student services hub for guidance.

Students access support in many contexts — on phones between lectures, with screen readers, or under stress when clarity matters most. Accessible design ensures everyone can find help quickly, understand content, and use forms and navigation without unnecessary barriers.
