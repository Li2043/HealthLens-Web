Mental Wellbeing

Resources and practical signposting for stress, loneliness, sleep, academic pressure and knowing where to ask for support.

What you can find here

  • Signposting for academic pressure, stress and study-related worries
  • Resources for loneliness, belonging and building connections
  • Links to university wellbeing services and NHS self-help guidance
  • Urgent mental health support routes when you need help quickly

This page is for signposting and general wellbeing information. If you need urgent help, use official urgent support routes.

Common situations

  • You feel overwhelmed by coursework, exams or academic deadlines
  • You feel lonely or disconnected after moving to a new country
  • Your sleep or daily routine has been disrupted by stress
  • You want to book a university wellbeing appointment
  • You need urgent mental health support but it is not an immediate emergency

Academic pressure and stress

Coursework, assessments and adapting to a new academic system can be stressful. Bristol SU and the University offer academic and wellbeing advice routes. Contacting support early can help you understand your options.

View Bristol SU academic advice (opens in new tab)

Loneliness and belonging

Moving abroad can feel isolating at first. Student groups, societies and community activities can help you build connections. See the Community & Sport page for more signposting.

Explore community and sport resources

Sleep and routine

Changes in environment, time zones and study pressure can affect sleep and daily routines. NHS Every Mind Matters offers general self-help guidance — not personalised medical advice.

Visit NHS Every Mind Matters (opens in new tab)

University wellbeing support

The University of Bristol offers wellbeing appointments and support for students who want guidance or a conversation about what they are going through.

Book a wellbeing appointment (opens in new tab)

Urgent mental health support

If you need urgent mental health support but it is not an immediate emergency, NHS 111 can direct you to the right service. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 999 or go to A&E.

Samaritans offers confidential listening support — call 116 123 free, any time.
