Registering with a GP

Most students should register with a GP near where they live. University of Bristol students may be able to register with the Students' Health Service if they are in the practice area.

Registering early makes it easier to book appointments and access non-urgent care. Official guidance explains eligibility, what to bring and how registration works.

Pharmacy, NHS 111 and emergency routes

Pharmacists can advise on common minor illnesses and medicines questions. NHS 111 can help when you need medical guidance now but it is not life-threatening. Call 999 or go to A&E for emergencies.

HealthLens does not assess symptoms or tell you where to go. If you are unsure, use NHS 111. If someone is in immediate danger, call 999.

Everyday health in the UK

Understanding how the NHS works can help you know where to look for official information. This includes GP appointments, prescriptions, referrals and follow-up care.

UKCISA and the NHS provide practical explanations for international students. Always check the original source for the latest details.

Care options guide

This guide explains common UK healthcare support routes and links to official information. It does not assess symptoms or decide where you should go. If you are unsure, use NHS 111. If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call 999 or go to A&E.

Please note: This student project signposts to official services. It does not provide medical advice, diagnosis, treatment recommendations, emergency response, or symptom assessment.

Pharmacy First: common conditions

NHS Pharmacy First may allow pharmacists to support some common conditions where clinically appropriate. Suitability can depend on age, symptoms and clinical judgement. A pharmacist can advise, treat where appropriate, or refer to a GP or another healthcare professional.

  • impetigo
  • infected insect bites
  • earache
  • sore throat
  • sinusitis
  • urinary tract infections
  • shingles

This list is for signposting only. It does not mean a pharmacy is always the right option. If you are unsure, use NHS 111 or contact a healthcare professional.
