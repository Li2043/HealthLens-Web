Budgeting basics

Planning your income and essential costs — rent, food, travel and bills — can help you avoid financial stress. MoneyHelper and UCAS offer free, impartial budgeting guidance.

Read MoneyHelper budgeting guidance (opens in new tab)

Student bank accounts

Most students open a UK bank account for rent, wages and everyday spending. Compare official guidance and check eligibility requirements before applying.

International students should also read UKCISA finance guidance for context on fees and funding.

Bills and recurring costs

Recurring costs may include rent, energy, phone, transport and subscriptions. Tracking these separately from one-off purchases makes budgeting clearer.

See the Housing & Bills page for shared housing and utility signposting.

Student discounts and shopping

Student discounts, second-hand shopping and budgeting before buying can help manage everyday spending. This is not a deals page — HealthLens does not recommend specific products or include affiliate links.

Check source reliability and avoid impulse purchases when money is tight.

Scams and safe spending

Students can be targeted by scams about visas, fees, accommodation or part-time work. Be cautious with unexpected messages, upfront payments and offers that seem too good to be true.

Report suspicious activity to your bank and seek advice from official sources.

Emergency financial support

If you are struggling with money, contact your university money advice service early. They can signpost you to support options and explain what help may be available.
